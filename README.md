##### Simulation Overview



**What the Simulation Does**



My simulation is a 2D grid that shows the Sun in the middle, with an elongated area beneath it that shows how the Sun's mass is making something like a hole. There is also the Earth rotating around the Sun. Every time someone increases the mass of the Sun, the Earth immediately goes farther away from the Sun. When someone decreases the mass, the Earth rotates around the Sun closer to it.



When the mass is increased a lot, the body goes far away from the Sun and does not rotate around it anymore. It becomes as big as the Sun and goes far away from it to create its own hole where other planets can revolve around it.



My general idea for the simulation is based on Einstein's theory of spacetime. The purpose is to allow people to explore a concept that is difficult to observe directly by making it something they can see and interact with.



**Target Users**



The target users are students, children, and anyone interested in learning about Einstein's theory of spacetime. The simulation is especially for people who may understand concepts better by seeing and interacting with them instead of only reading or listening to an explanation.



**Key Interactions**



The main controls allow the user to interact with the simulation and change what happens.



\- \*\*M:\*\* Increases the mass of the Sun.

\- \*\*N:\*\* Decreases the mass of the Sun.

\- \*\*W, A, S, D:\*\* Allows the user to rotate around the 2D grid using the first-person perspective.

\- \*\*I:\*\* Shows captions and explanations at the bottom of the screen.

\- \*\*Delete:\*\* Removes the captions.

\- \*\*Audio:\*\* The user can listen to explanations while interacting with the simulation.



The user can therefore change the mass, move around the simulation, view explanations, and listen to audio while observing how the simulation changes.
